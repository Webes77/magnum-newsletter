01 The Newsline

ChatGPT can now read your iCloud email and diary

Why it matters. Plenty of sole traders run on an iCloud address. ChatGPT can now read that inbox and diary, so it can tell you what needs an answer first.

What to do. Try it on reading only, on a quiet day. Ask what needs a reply, and press send yourself on anything it drafts.

My take It's handing a new hire the office keys. Fine for the front desk, not the safe. Let it read for a month before you let it near anything else.

iCloud Mail is Apple's free email. OpenAI has added plugins that let ChatGPT read your iCloud Mail and Calendar. Reports say they work on free and paid plans and are rolling out worldwide, so yours may not have them yet.

To connect, you make an app-specific password in your Apple account, a one-off code that works like a spare key. Mail and diary connect separately.

One catch. Reports say that key opens contacts too, not only mail. Delete it on Apple's side to cut access.

02 Looking Sideways

Ads made by AI sold worse, even when nobody could tell

Why it matters. AI-made posts and ads are cheap and tempting. In a US test, shoppers rated the human-made versions higher, even though most couldn't tell which was which.

What to do. Use AI for first drafts and ideas. Then put something real in: your own photo, your own job, your own words.

My take AI is a good apprentice and a poor face of the business. Customers can't name what's missing, but they notice when it isn't there.

The test covered 20 ads from 10 brands, shown to 3,000 US consumers. Human-made ads scored 11 points above the benchmark. AI-made ads scored five below it. Only about 13 percent of people who saw an AI ad were even somewhat confident it was AI.

It's an American study, but shoppers are the same on the Gold Coast. A separate, larger study found AI and human ads got similar click rates, so the gap is in what people do next.

03 Tool of the Week

Apple puts an AI helper inside Pages, Numbers and Keynote

Why it matters. If your quotes, price lists or slides live in Apple's apps, AI help is built in, with no copying into another tool.

What to do. If you use a Mac or iPad, start the free month on one real job, like a price list, and cancel if it doesn't earn its keep.

My take Worth a trial if you already live in Apple's apps. If you don't, nothing here is a reason to switch.

Pages, Numbers and Keynote stay free. The AI features need Apple's Creator Studio subscription, A$19.99 a month or A$199 a year including GST, with a one-month free trial for new subscribers.

The assistant arrives in November as a beta. It drafts in Pages, sorts figures and builds charts in Numbers, and animates slides in Keynote. Apple says it works in US English only for now, so check that before you pay.

04 Prompt of the Week

Sort your inbox into reply today, reply soon and ignore

Why it matters. Most inboxes hide two or three emails that matter under thirty that don't. This finds them in a minute.

What to do. Copy your last twenty emails and your diary into the prompt, read what it flags, and send the replies yourself.

My take A good office manager doesn't answer your mail for you. They put the right three emails on your desk. That's the job here.

It works in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Pasting the emails in is the safer start, because nothing is connected to your account.

Prompt

You are a calm, sharp office manager for a small business. I run [your business].

I will paste my recent emails and my diary. Tell me what needs my attention and in what order, so I can clear my inbox in 15 minutes.

Work only from what I paste. If something is unclear, say so instead of guessing, because a wrong guess costs me a customer. Anything about money, bank details or a change of payment details gets marked "check by phone", because those are the emails scammers send.

Keep it short, plain and direct. No cheerfulness.

Use three headings: Reply today, Reply soon, Safe to ignore. Under each, one line per email: who it's from, what they want, and the next step.

Then write a two-sentence draft reply for each email under Reply today. Finish with the one email I should answer first, and why. You draft and I send, so write nothing as if it has already gone.

My emails: [paste your emails here]
My diary: [paste your diary here]

Use it for
Copy the prompt into any AI chat.
Replace the three bracketed parts with your business, your emails and your diary.
Read the drafts, change anything that isn't how you'd say it, and send them yourself.

06 The Magnum

A plumber in a toy pack, from one prompt

Why it matters. The toy-in-a-box photo is one of the most shared AI picture styles of 2026, and any trade can copy it.

What to do. Swap the plumber for your own trade or your best product, then use the picture on a post.

My take The packaging tells the story. A spare part, a barcode and a tick badge do more than a clever caption.

Setup:

Several guides call the toy-figure look one of the most shared AI photo styles of 2026. Swap the plumber for your own trade. Keep any words in the picture to a few, because image tools still fumble spelling.

Prompt

A boxed collectible action figure of a friendly Australian plumber in a blue work shirt and hi-vis vest, holding a small pipe wrench, sealed in a cardboard-backed plastic blister pack hanging on a pegboard in a hardware shop. A spare tap washer sits in a second compartment beside the figure. The cardboard backing has a bold red and navy header block with the invented business name "Reliable Pipes Co", a simple tick badge and a tiny barcode in one corner. Product photography, front-on, 50mm lens, soft shop lighting, shallow depth of field, crisp reflections on the plastic. Mood: cheerful, collectible and a little cheeky. No real person, no real brand, no other text.

Take from it:
Pick one thing about your business, make it a character in a pack, and you have a post people stop for.

Paste your last twenty emails into any AI with the prompt above and see which one it says to answer first.
