The Newsline

ChatGPT can now build you a working calculator, and plenty of people can try it for free

On 7 October, OpenAI released GPT-6 and a feature it calls Intelligent UI in ChatGPT.

Instead of a block of text, an answer can now include forms, tappable buttons, charts and small tools you use inside the chat.

The useful part for a business owner is simple. Describe a calculator in plain English and you get a working one.

Paid plans got it on 7 October. OpenAI says free users get it too. It rolls out account by account, so it may not have reached yours yet.

It works in the Chat tab. OpenAI says the Pro reasoning option and older ChatGPT desktop apps don't support it.

Here's the catch. A calculator that looks right can still be wrong.

So test it before you trust it. Type in a job where you already know the answer, and check the total matches. Then change one number and check again.

Today: pick one job you work out by hand every week, like a quote or a supplier order. The Prompt of the Week below builds the calculator and checks it for you. Ten minutes.

The Win

A café owner stopped running out of milk on Saturdays

I see this one so often I've told it as a pattern, not as one client.

A café owner wrote the supplier order every Thursday night, from memory, after a twelve-hour day.

Some Saturdays she ran out of milk mid-morning. Other weeks a box of avocados went in the bin.

Her memory wasn't the problem. Thursday night was. Nobody does good maths after close.

So now she hands AI three things instead: the last four weeks of orders, a photo of the cool room, and the weekend bookings.

Then one line: "Draft the next order. Flag any item that's well above or below normal, and tell me why."

It came back with the order and two flags. Milk looked short for a weekend with a big group booked. Avocados were about double what she'd used the week before.

She topped up the milk, cut the avocados, and sent it. A few minutes, and she trusted it more than her Thursday-night guess.

The AI doesn't know her café better than she does. It just never gets tired, and it checks every line against the numbers. She still makes the call.

Try it on your next order. Paste in a month of orders, add anything unusual coming up, and ask it to flag what looks off.

Tool of the Week

DigUp

You know the file exists. You just can't remember what you called it.

DigUp is an app for Mac, Windows and Linux that searches your own files by what's in them, not by what they're named.

You type what you remember, like "the photo of the cracked tile" or "the quote with the long lead time", and it looks through photos, audio, video and documents for a match.

It does all of this on your own computer. Nothing is sent to the cloud. The first set-up downloads a model of about 865 MB, once.

On a Mac it needs Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later.

It's free and open source. It's also new and changing quickly, so try it on one test folder before you point it at everything.

Worth a go if your files are a mess and you'd rather not send them anywhere.

DigUp: https://github.com/ARahim3/DigUp

Prompt of the Week

Turn a job you work out by hand into a calculator

Most owners have one calculation they do over and over, on paper or in their head.

This prompt turns it into a small calculator that you can use again and again.

It also makes the AI check its own work against a job you already know the answer to.

Prompt

You are a careful adviser who builds simple calculators for small business owners who are not technical.

Here is a job I work out by hand every time: [the job you work out by hand, for example quoting a job or pricing an order].

The numbers I type in each time are: [the numbers you enter each time].

These prices, rates and rules never change, so build them in: [your fixed prices, rates and rules]. Use only these. If you need a figure I have not given you, ask me before you build, because a guessed price in a quote costs me real money.

Here is one job I have already worked out by hand, with the answer I know is right: [one example with its correct total].

Build me a small working calculator inside this chat. Put a box for each number I type in at the top, the breakdown line by line under it, and the total at the bottom. Keep the wording plain.

When it is built, do three things. First, enter my example and tell me whether your total matches mine. Second, tell me which figure or rule you were least sure of. Third, stop and wait while I try it.

Use it for
Fill in the four square brackets with your own job, your inputs, your fixed prices and one worked example.
Paste it into ChatGPT or Claude.
When it replies, try a second job you know the answer to before you rely on it.

The Magnum

The kelp harvest

Setup:

Nothing to do with the news. Just a striking image built from a single written prompt: a night-blue sea, a farmer and a net of glowing kelp.

Prompt

A kelp farmer in yellow oilskins hauls a glowing net of bioluminescent kelp over the side of a small wooden boat at blue hour, the sea behind them lit green from below. Wide 35mm documentary photograph, low angle from the waterline, shallow depth of field, a little motion in the waves. Quiet and awed, cool teal against warm amber, the hush before sunrise.

Take from it:
Three parts, in this order: who is doing what, how the camera sees it, and the mood. Change only the mood and keep the rest, and you'll see how much work that last line does.

Pick one calculation you do by hand and run the prompt above.

Then enter a job you already know the answer to, and check the total.

If it matches, you've got a tool. If it doesn't, you've found the mistake before a customer did.
